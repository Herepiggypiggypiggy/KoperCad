Release candidate 8 of the KoperCAD beta. Windows 10/11 only. No admin rights are needed.

## Files

- `KoperCAD-*-setup.exe`: per-user installer (installs under your profile).
- `KoperCAD-*-portable.zip`: unzip anywhere and double-click `run.cmd`; includes `kopercad-cli.exe`. A file `kopercad.portable` next to the exe keeps settings, logs and recovery files in the `data` folder beside it.
- `SHA256SUMS`: checksums of the two files above. `BUILD_INFO.txt`: version, commit and build time.

## Try these first

1. **Installer and first start (needs a real Windows PC).** Install without any admin prompt. Start from the Start menu and by double-clicking a project file. Check Help > About (maker, licence, "Copy version info", "Open log folder", the graphics adapter line). If the window stays black or the program does not start, use the Start menu entry **KoperCAD (safe graphics)** (portable zip: `run-safe-graphics.cmd`). Uninstall once and read the question about removing settings, logs and recovery files: the default is No, and your projects are never deleted. Install a second build over a running copy.
2. **New in rc8: Wire-it, the wiring pass one wire at a time.** In 3D, Layers > **Wire-it** (or View > 3D > Wire-it, or Ctrl+K "wire-it"). Each step lights both ends and the routed path of one connection and gives the cut length with the end allowance, strip, ferrule, labels and how full each duct gets after that wire; ticks are saved in the project (one undo step each) and a half-wired cabinet resumes where it stopped. Reports > Export > **Export the Wire-it list (PDF)** prints it. Cable cores read "run cable", not cut, and are left out of the cut total. Wire-it and Build order switch each other off. The wire class (earth, power, supply, signal) and the duct fill are models of usual practice, not readings.
3. **New in rc8: Kitting and stock optimiser.** Before the first cut: Reports > Bench output > **Kitting** tab, Reports > Export > **Export kitting list (PDF)** (Ctrl+K "kitting"), and "3 Kitting list.pdf" in the Workshop pack. It packs rail and duct cuts into the bar lengths you buy (saw cut, mixed lengths, offcuts on hand used first), totals hookup wire per size and colour onto reels, counts ferrules, lugs, screws and label sheets with spares, prints a pick list per build phase and one shopping list merged with the parts list. Type your own stock (one item per line) and see that it is used first. Bar and reel lengths, saw cut, allowances and packs are planning values, printed in the notes.
4. **New in rc8: Commissioning Test tab.** Commissioning has a fourth tab, **Test** (the old "Punch list" tab is now called **Punch**). Every drawn wire is listed in wiring order with an expected continuity limit and an insulation minimum; type the readings you take at the bench and a miss opens a punch item; **Write test sheet PDF** prints the sheet. A note explains why the test sheet counts drawn wires (27 in the example) while the cut list and Wire-it count connections (25). It is a design aid, not a compliance test record.
5. **New in rc8: Work cards, further.** Run it again on a cabinet you printed in rc7: wire groups have a cut-and-label strip, every wire card has its own mark ("Control (1 of 3)"), so a card printed earlier reads **CHANGED since print** when the design moves on, and the cover shows revision and print date; cable cores say "run cable =A1-W1, 12020 mm" and the tools row counts the cut wires; sizes print as mm2; the label cut-out template axes and the drilling template (sorted drill table, larger rail strip) are improved. A new project clears the old print record.
6. **New in rc8: EMC markers and lessons.** The EMC views on the Cabinet plate and in 3D now also mark motor cable shield (EMC-04), shield pigtail (EMC-05) and door (EMC-12) findings (View > EMC). Learn has new lessons: Cooling advice, Cooling what-if, Cooler layout, 3D Simulate run, plus work cards and EMC fix-it from rc7 (62 lessons now). Schematic potential feed names (L1, PE, 24V) move off their neighbours.
7. **New in rc7: Work cards for hand work.** The Build order as half-page workshop cards in one PDF, two per A4: a cover with the tool list, a tick sheet, one card per step, wire cards with cut lengths, the strip length per end ("typ." is a planning value, the datasheet of the clamp or ferrule wins) and the end allowance, rail cards that draw every device clipped on the rail at its distance from the named rail end, duct cards with the outline corner and fixing positions, cut-out cards for vents, fans, grommets, slots and cable entries, tick-off cards for supply and field wires outside the plate, and a label card that names the label stock. Find it: Reports > Export > Export work cards (PDF), 3D > Layers > Build order > Work cards (PDF), Reports > Bench output (the Workshop pack adds "1 Work cards.pdf"). **Print this step** on the Build order strip writes one step as a single A4 PDF next to the project. Print at 100% and check the 100 mm ruler. Cut-out cards come first: do them before the plate goes in, and check the first cut against the real cabinet.
8. **New in rc7: EMC fix-it.** In Check > EMC check, on the schematic cable card (View > EMC net classes) and in the 3D and plate views, a finding can offer a fix with **Preview** and **Apply**. The preview runs on a copy of the project and shows findings before and after (for example "5 before, 5 after (one eased)") and the risk change; **Cancel** changes nothing; **Apply** is one undo step; the fix can be drawn before and after in 3D and on the plate. Fixes: route a wire in another duct (EMC-01), route two wires in ducts at least N mm apart, move a filter next to its drive (EMC-07), move a sensitive device away from a drive (EMC-17) or out of the field of a wire (EMC-19), and the door strap for devices on the door and wires across the hinge (EMC-12, the twelfth rule). **Save EMC record (PDF / Excel)** in Check > EMC check writes the findings with what to do at the bench, risk scores, assumptions, the not-checked list, date, program version and a review block.
9. **New in rc7: Cooling what-if.** The Heat strip button **Cooling what-if** (Cabinet or 3D), the plate right-click and the command palette open a wizard: you name the hottest day and duty, the model ranks fixes (move devices along rails, the best passive or powered vent or fan pair, layout plus openings, a bigger enclosure as model only), previews the picked fix in 3D, shows a before/after table with **Copy the table**, and **Apply** (moves are one undo step; openings go into Vents and fans and undo there). It is a model, not a measurement.
10. **Build mode.** Step through building a cabinet at the bench: one step per device with its position on the plate, door, wall, roof or floor (door measured from the hinge edge), ticks with name and time, claiming a step per person, a problem editor with photos, and "Print sheet..." for a build sheet PDF. Desk and tablet should run the same build.
11. **Machine options.** Mark parts of a project as options: left-out objects appear ghosted on the schematic, View > Layers has a switch, and the Option column shows in the Reports lists, the PDF, the generated report pages, the Excel export and the handover package (the command-line tool does not carry it yet). Cabinet, heat and Commissioning follow the chosen options.
12. **Review notes.** Comments on pages and objects with open/resolved status, replies, search, an "N1" bubble on the canvas and in the exported PDF, and a review-notes list. The handover package only includes the review notes if you tick them (off by default, so internal remarks do not reach the customer).
13. **3D heat: vents and fans.** The Vents and fans panel draws openings in the walls, lets the fans reach the solver, colours the airflow by speed, saves the openings in the project, and lets you drag them in 3D. Cooling advice offers a fan together with its grille (a small existing grille no longer hides the proposed one, and heat labels near the limit read "at its limit"); the **Cooler layout** button in the Heat panel opens the Cabinet view and suggests a placement that takes the heat into account.
14. **EMC design aid.** Check > EMC check, plus EMC views in the 3D cabinet and on the cabinet plate: keep-clear bands, a low-frequency magnetic field estimate (a model) and a field slice. It is switched on per project.
15. **Ask Claude as co-engineer (optional).** A draft workspace with plan and question cards, 17 actions, all undoable. Needs your own key: see the caveats.
16. **Large projects.** Open a project with several hundred pages: Commissioning, the bench packet, Reports counts and the page order should stay responsive. Reports counts show "-" until the count for the current state is known.
17. **Learn.** The Learn window is now a suite with a side rail, a home with a recommended lesson, topics, search and level chips, and more than ten new lessons (11 in one batch, more since), including a Machines track. Ctrl+K "continue lesson" resumes where you stopped. New in rc7: lessons for EMC net classes (ending with Preview, then Apply), EMC on the plate and Build order, and a Current paths step in the Live view lesson. They are checked by replay and pictures, not walked by a person, and the Dutch text has had no native review.
18. **Cabinet layout.** Place all now takes heat into account (the preview may move hot parts and says how many were placed differently). Rails run through the middle of their devices and there is a "Centre rails on devices" button. Rows and ducts follow the 35 mm rail band.
19. **Since rc4 (rc5 and later): look and feel.** Preferences > Contrast: Standard or High, for dark and light. Hover a wire row or cable row in the outline, or a relation card in the Inspector, and the item lights up on the drawing. Jumps on the 2D canvases glide for 220 ms. Choosing a problem in the Issues list (click or F8) highlights its row softly and scrolls to it. Quiet toasts fade. All of these stand still with **Reduce motion**.
20. **Since rc4 (rc5 and later): drawing and live view.** The wire tool shows a small label naming the pin, junction or wire your next click connects to; a refused click leaves a thin cross and the reason. Live view marks the wires on a current path with thin dashes (a model of where current runs, not how much or which way; the **Current paths** checkbox turns it off). Placing the Three-phase supply into a drawing with no supply adds the L1 L2 L3 N PE feeds in the same undo step (see item 17 for the wires to a starter). When the last error or warning is fixed, one plain line says "No errors or warnings left".
21. **Since rc4 (rc5 and later): cabinet and heat.** A short ring and bore cue marks the holes an edit drills in the plate, in the Cabinet view and in 3D. Fans turn and air crosses the openings in the 3D Heat view; the motion only illustrates direction, its speed is not the air speed; a small slow fan icon sits beside a proposed fan in Cooling selection. Several small grilles add up, a big flow gets a wide grille along the wall, and the heat colour bar ticks are round steps. The PLC Add card and I/O list let you choose the address style.
22. **New in rc6: Live view and the supply macro.** A held pushbutton now stays pressed for as long as you hold it (before, it sprang back after about a second); the Operate buttons and pole badges sense click and drag, so a click that moves past the drag threshold no longer counts as a click. Placing the Three-phase supply with a starter now draws the wires from L1 L2 L3, the control breaker and PE to the starter's open entries, and joins N to the coil return, so Live view shows a neutral through the coils. It joins only when exactly one candidate entry is open, and an unrelated macro joins nothing. Try holding the start button for several seconds on real hardware.
23. **New in rc6: EMC on the plate and in the schematic.** Net classes show on the schematic (EMC overlay); the EMC cable card in the schematic panel lists shield ends and findings with one-click shield fixes, and each finding says what its level means (a rule that holds or not, an estimate, or a field value that is not a measurement). On the Cabinet plate, View > EMC has chips **Bonding** and **Shields**: bonding outlines with a glowing spot on a weak or missing bond, and shield-end rings in three states. The 3D EMC view has a **Bonding** chip, and the engine now knows device heights and case-to-plate bonding links.
24. **New in rc6: 3D view and Cooling advice.** A part picked in the Cabinet is flown to when 3D opens. Plate and rails get a faint metal finish with a switch in Layers back to flat colours. **Build order** steps through rails, ducts, devices and wires. Cooling advice sizes fans from your own fan parts and the cooling list and names the article, puts the 3D run next to the 2D prediction of the pair you added, and its window sits beside the cabinet with the fan icon on fan options.
25. **New in rc6: text and Commissioning.** Device tags search for the first free place when their library place is covered by wires, symbols, text, the frame or the sheet edge, and a rotated terminal column drops its strip name clear of a device above. In Commissioning and Build mode a step claimed by someone who has not set a name reads "taken" instead of "you".
26. **Workshop pack, wire numbering, parts import, Single-line diagram, Standards report, Cable runs in 3D.** As in rc3, if you did not try them yet.

## Changes you will notice

- **Installer cleanup (since rc7).** The build server now proves the file types, the update over leftovers and the silent uninstall: uninstall removes empty `.kopercad` and `.novo` keys, and "Also remove KoperCAD data from this PC?" (default No) also clears old NovoElectric data folders. Projects are never deleted. There is still no guard against starting a second copy. The pass by hand on a fresh Windows account is still to do (`docs/WINDOWS-CLEAN-PROFILE.md`).
- **The project file extension is now `.kopercad`.** Files named `.novo` from older builds still open, and saving writes `.kopercad`. Earlier test builds cannot list `.kopercad` files in their Open dialog.
- **Delete the Starter folder** in My macros (or Company macros) and reopen the macro browser, to get the fully wired Starter macros. An installed copy is never replaced automatically.
- **Duct placement changed.** Ducts now go over the rail and rows have a minimum gap of 57.5 mm. In a nearly full plate a separation may be dropped or a device left unplaced; both are reported. Plans saved earlier keep the old rail position until you run Place all again, and sheets printed before this change should be printed again.
- **Power-wire heat in ducts.** A planning upper value: copper at the insulation maximum temperature, no diversity unless you state it, neutral and PE not counted, and part-load scaling is linear, so it overestimates.
- **Wire production data.** The wire list and bench output carry bundle, sequence, track, direction, end processing, D-names and sleeve A/B.
- **Live view** says when no supply is defined. **Ctrl+K** finds "cut-out", "drill template" and "door" (Export workshop pack), "duct heat" and "wire heat", slotted ducts, and the PLC input card.
- **About > Check for updates** is a manual button. It makes one request to GitHub, sends no project data, and ignores release candidates (it says "No newer stable version found"), so it will not find a newer rc. The real GitHub answer, the offline text and proxy behaviour on Windows have not been seen.
- **Report tables** size their columns to the text. The PLC I/O list import keeps page names.
- **A recovered copy of a damaged project** keeps the damaged file's extension.
- **Live view holds pushbuttons longer** and a click that drifts past the drag threshold is a drag, not a click.
- **High contrast** is new: heavier strokes and palettes whose text and drawing colours are checked against WCAG AA contrast ratios in tests. Standard Light now uses the light potential colours in Live view (it used dark ones before).
- Files written by earlier builds are tested to still open and edit.

## Caveats

- **Not code-signed.** Windows SmartScreen may warn on first start: choose "More info", then "Run anyway".
- **Clean-profile Windows installer pass still not done.** The installer has been run only on the Windows build server (per-user install, no admin rights, the app starts). A first install on a fresh Windows profile, with the real questions and a real uninstall, has not been seen by a person.
- **Not verified on real Windows hardware.** The automated checks run on Linux and on a Windows build server without a GPU. The installer is checked there (per-user install, no admin rights, the app starts), but the new installer questions, update over a running copy, portable `data` folder, the safe-graphics renderer, double-click opening, HiDPI at 150% and 200% and the absence of a console flash can only be proven on a real PC. Most of the new screens (Build mode, Review notes, Vents, EMC, Learn suite) have only been seen in Linux screenshots. Two new interactions were tested with replay and unit tests only, not with a real keyboard and mouse: the Place-wheel route in a lesson and Page Down with the pointer over the lesson card.
- **Ask Claude needs your own API key and has only been tested with a scripted model**, not the real API. It is off until you turn it on, it sends the data scope you allow plus what you press, and every edit it suggests can be undone. The steps for a real-key test are in `docs/CLAUDE-ASSISTANT-TEST.md`.
- **EMC is a rule-based design aid, not a test result and not a compliance statement.** Twelve of the 20 planned rules (EMC-01 to EMC-20) are built; the rest are listed under "Not checked". Distances and field levels are order-of-magnitude estimates with stated assumptions, the IEC 61000-4-8 levels are unverified, and the plate bands are drawn at most a fifth of the plate's short side (the finding card shows the real planning distance).
- **Heat, airflow and cooling results are models, not measurements.** The 2D heat model, the 3D CFD (a reduced model for large cabinets) and the cooler layout use planning values and are not validated against a real enclosure. Openings in the 3D panel are labelled "(model)". Grille sizing is a planning rule (1.5 m/s, a single grille at most 400 mm, so very large flows need a second one); several small grilles now add up, and the wording near a device's limit reads "at its limit".
- **Wire sizes, colours, breaking capacity checks and standard clause numbers are planning values.** The IEC 60204-1 clause numbers are the authors' reading, not checked against a licensed copy. The short-circuit figures use the supply data you state (the example's 400 V / 6 kA is illustrative only) and leave out motor contribution and busbar impedance.
- **Build mode:** Large mode below 1200 pt has no picture; a tick you undid can return from the desk copy when copies merge; grouping power, control and earth is usual practice, not read from the schematic. The bubble for a review note sits on the page frame corner and the delete bin has no label.
- **Machine options are not complete everywhere yet.** The Cabinet plate keeps left-out spots reserved, while heat, cooling advice and cooler layout treat left-out devices as absent, so a proposed layout can overlap a reserved spot. The title block still counts a left-out page ("Sheet 1 of 3"). The command-line tool (`kopercad-cli`) does not carry the Option column.
- **Work cards: positions are the cabinet model's, not a measured plate**, and every card says so. True size depends on the print dialog (print at 100%); the cut-out cards are drawn at 1:2 and say "NOT true size" with a true-size ruler, and the single step card is a 1:2.5 template. None of it has been printed on a real Windows PC. The cover lists cut-list wires that have no end in the plated cabinet (supply and field wires) and files them under the first cabinet by default when several cabinets exist; that note was tested by text only, not with a two-cabinet project. Duct fixing positions (25 mm from each end, at most 200 mm apart, on the centre line of the duct base) are a labelled planning rule, the duct maker's instructions win, and the holes are not in the drilling list. Grille cards give no horizontal position yet. The single card reads "STEP 2 of 42" while the strip reads "Step 2 of 17" (workshop steps against part steps), and the same mark in two cabinets overwrites the step file. Work cards (PDF) asks where to save; the generic Export PDF still writes to Documents\KoperCAD.
- **Wire-it, Kitting and the Commissioning Test tab are new and unseen on real Windows hardware**: the 3D strip, the Kitting and Test tabs and their PDFs have only been seen in Linux pictures and never printed on a real PC. Wire-it: the wire class and the duct fill are models, a T-junction's legs are separate steps, a step with no path on this plate (a feed, a wire to another cabinet, a cable core, an unplaced device) has no picture, and the order of the wires is a usual-practice order, not a standard. Kitting: bar and reel lengths, saw cut, allowances and packs are planning values; estimated wire lengths are flagged; devices and wires without a part or cross-section are named as making the list incomplete; known small wording items remain ("x % held back" although the allowance is added to every wire, the column "Bar piece mm", "1 reels", long For cells on a plate with many rails). Test tab: the continuity limits and insulation minimums are planning defaults, no standard is cited, and the sheet is a design aid, not a compliance test record; at 1366x768 the Test tab is cut off by the dock itself and is reached by scrolling.
- **Work cards after rc7:** a card printed earlier reads CHANGED only against the print record of this session (the record is not saved in the project), a new project clears it, and the cards have not been printed on a real Windows PC. The rc7 notes about 100% print size, cut-out cards at 1:2 and the step numbers on the single card still apply.
- **The new lessons** (Cooling advice, Cooling what-if, Cooler layout, 3D Simulate run) are checked by replay and pictures, not walked by a person; the Dutch text has had no native review. At 1366x768 with a lesson card up the 3D Heat panel is short (scroll it or minimise the card). The text placement of terminal numbers still crowds on rotated 4 to 6 mm rows and on PE/N mixed strips; there is no automatic text-overlap check, so those verdicts came from pictures.
- **EMC fix-it** is a rule-based design aid, never compliance. A fix can change the route and cut length of a wire (reprint the wire list, the cut list and the drilling plan), can clear one finding and bring another (in the example, the other-duct fix clears EMC-01 and brings EMC-02), and is refused when no duct pair or free spot allows it. The preview says that heat, airflow, door swing and wire length are not checked. The move search is capped at 400 full layout checks; a very crowded plate can be refused although a free spot exists far away, and the preview says so when it stops at the cap. The wording of the preview for a fix that lowers the risk without clearing a finding is still being refined. The EMC record has no save dialog yet; the Excel file never replaces an earlier file (it gets "... 2.xlsx") and the PDF replaces our own earlier PDF. The door rule (EMC-12) is a rule of practice, and IEC clause numbers stay unverified.
- **Cooling what-if is a model, not a measurement.** It uses the 2D heat model with planning values, the enclosure is not validated, and the 2D slice sees no depth. First place means the easiest fix that clears every limit (it says "thin margin" under 2 K), not an optimum, and a bigger enclosure is never applied for you. A combined moves-plus-openings Apply is two undo histories (the text says so). The 2D model now includes the fan jet speed at device surfaces: it was fitted on one box and checked on two others with one device and a 60 to 250 m3/h fan (the 3D to 2D gain ratio over all 36 cases was 1.01 at the median, between 0.69 and 1.28, with the 2D gain too high by up to 45% in the deepest box and too low by up to 22% in the widest), and beyond that it is an extrapolation: further along the stream the 2D gain was 0.8 to 5 times the 3D one, beside the stream the 3D solver gave a third to three quarters of the gain shown, and in one wide box the device ran about 4 K hotter with the fan on. A fan fix can therefore be overstated (by up to 45% in a deeper box, and by more further along the stream) or understated, so check it with Simulate in 3D. Fan numbers in the what-if and in Cooling advice differ from builds before rc7. The ranking agreed with the 3D solver on 3 of 3 pairs, which is model against model. At 1366x768 the what-if window covers the Vents and fans panel until folded and its list is about 175 px and scrolls.
- **Rc6 additions are unseen on Windows hardware** and have only Linux pictures: EMC on the plate and in the schematic, the cable card, Build order, the metal finish, the Cooling advice window and the held pushbutton. The held-pushbutton test in particular needs a real mouse and a real hold.
- **Cooling advice window at 1366x768 in 3D:** it sits beside the cabinet and scrolls its comparison block, but it still partly covers "? Mouse and keys" and most of the Heat panel while open. The comparison is two unvalidated models side by side, not a measurement.
- **EMC bonding and shield rings** follow the plate finish and bonding you state; they are rule-based, never a measured contact. The outline is drawn 8 mm clear of the case and is only a thin line at 1366x768. The metal finish is only a look.
- **Text placement:** 224 tags on the densest stress pages still have no free place within 12 mm, and the PE block's own tag over its first terminal is still open.
- **Supply macro:** N is joined only to a starter's control side (coil return); after placing, the status line reads "No errors or warnings left" instead of the "Placed ... wires joined" message.
- **New look-and-feel items are unseen on Windows hardware** and were checked in Linux pictures, mostly at 1366x768 (the Issues highlight has no 1920x1080 picture). High contrast light: the Live view colours in it are not reviewed. The fan icon also turns for a proposed fan that is not in the cabinet yet (only its hover says so), and repaints about 30 times a second while shown. Live-path dashes show where, not how much or which way.
- **Review notes are internal.** They are not in the handover file unless you tick them (off by default).
- **Large-project speed.** The faster Reports counts, wire jobs and page order are proven by tests that give the same results; the speed gain was not re-measured on a real project, and the "-" in the Reports counts has no tooltip confirmed yet.
- **Safety symbols carry no safety category or PL/SIL evaluation.**
- Two wire-colour pixel checks and the `novo-cfd` simulation tests do not run on the Windows build server (no GPU; Linux covers the simulation tests). Time budgets in the large-project tests are wall-clock.
